### What the patch does
PR #16814 (commit ba8abaa) changed how Phoenix colors evaluator scores. Scores used to be green or red on either side of a threshold. Now the color is graded by how far a score is from a pivot:
- The pivot is the threshold, or the midpoint of the bounds if there is no threshold.
- A score at the pivot is neutral.
- Scores outside the bounds are clamped.
- A side of the pivot with no bound gets full color.

The docs still describe the old pass/fail behavior. The logic lives in `getOptimizationValue` in `js/app/src/components/annotation/optimizationUtils.ts`.

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `docs/phoenix/evaluation/server-evals/code-evaluators.mdx` | Step 3 no longer says "threshold for pass/fail coloring". The bounds and threshold bullets are rewritten. A new section explains the pivot, neutral scores, clamping, unbounded sides and the no-color case. It adds one worked example and says how to get plain pass/fail coloring (a threshold with no bounds). |
| `docs/phoenix/datasets-and-experiments/how-to-experiments/running-experiments.mdx` | Adds one paragraph after the optimization-direction list: colors are graded, here is the pivot, and a link to the section above. |

### How you could explain it in an interview
> "Phoenix colors every evaluation score in its experiment views, so you can see at a glance which runs got better or worse. They changed that from a hard green/red split at a threshold to a graded scale, but the docs still said 'threshold equals pass/fail'. So someone setting a threshold of 0.8 would expect 0.8 to be green and would see it shown as neutral. I read the actual scoring function, wrote down the rules it follows (the pivot, clamping, what happens with no bounds), and then turned every sentence of the docs into an assertion against the real function before proposing it. In evaluation work, how a score is *displayed* is part of the metric. If people misread the colors, they make the wrong call on a regression. Treating documentation as something you test is a habit I bring to my own teams."
